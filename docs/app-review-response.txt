Hello App Review,

Thank you for your questions about TrueDepth usage in Casual Contacts. We have clarified the privacy policy and prepared a camera-mode explanation and permission controls in version 1.0.10 ([BUILD]).

1. Information processed
The app uses ARKit face tracking. It reads the tracked face transform, left and right eye transforms, camera transform, tracking status, and frame timestamps. From these it calculates the midpoint of the eyes and its position/distance relative to the screen. ARKit processes camera frames and face anchors locally. Our code does not read face mesh vertices, facial-expression blend shapes, gaze targets, or Face ID identity data.

2. Purpose
The only purpose is to render viewpoint-dependent perspective for layered contact cards. No face data is used for identification, authentication, profiling, advertising, or analytics. Camera mode is optional and off by default. Gyro-only motion remains available without camera permission.

3. Sharing, storage, retention, and deletion
No camera frames or face data are transmitted, shared with third parties, sold, or uploaded. They remain in volatile device memory during tracking. The app holds the latest derived viewpoint and a short gyro-orientation buffer for smooth rendering and temporary gyro continuation. These are cleared when tracking stops, including when camera mode is disabled, the user leaves the enabled card views, or the app becomes inactive. ARKit's retained session frames/anchors are released. Nothing is written to files, the contact database, photos, backups, or logs. There is no face-data retention between sessions and no persisted face data to delete. User-selected contact photos are a separate feature.

4. Privacy policy location
https://casualcontacts.app/privacy.html#face-data
Section 03a, “Face data and TrueDepth,” contains the subsections “Information processed,” “Purpose and consent,” “Sharing and disclosure,” and “Storage, retention, and deletion.” Section 03 describes permissions.

5. Exact privacy policy text
Information processed
“When you enable camera mode for the optional 3D scene, Apple ARKit and the TrueDepth/front camera process camera frames and a tracked face anchor. Casual Contacts reads the face transform, left and right eye transforms, camera transform, tracking status, and frame timestamps to calculate the midpoint of your eyes and its position and distance relative to the screen. The app does not read facial-expression blend shapes, face mesh vertices, gaze targets, or Face ID identity data.”

Purpose and consent
“This information is used only to animate the perspective of the layered contact cards. It is not used for identification, authentication, profiling, advertising, or analytics. Camera mode is optional and off by default. You can use gyro motion without camera access. The app explains camera use before requesting permission. If you decline the system permission request, cards use gyro motion. You can turn camera mode off in 3D scene or revoke Camera access in iOS Settings.”

Sharing and disclosure
“Camera frames and face data are not uploaded, transmitted, sold, or shared with any third party. No third-party service receives this information. Apple ARKit processes the data locally on the device.”

Storage, retention, and deletion
“Camera frames and derived face/eye positions exist only in volatile memory on your device during the active tracking session. They are not written to the contact database, files, photos, backups, or logs. The app retains the latest derived viewpoint and a short motion-orientation buffer to smooth animation and continue with gyro when the face leaves view. Tracking stops and this session data is cleared when you turn off camera mode or the 3D scene, leave the enabled card views, or the app becomes inactive. No face data is retained between sessions; there is no stored face data to delete. Photos you deliberately attach to a contact are separate from this feature and remain locally stored until you delete them or the record.”

Review steps
On a new installation, saving the first contact presents the optional camera-mode explanation once on supported hardware. Tap Continue to camera permission, then choose Allow or Don’t Allow in the system alert. Declining keeps gyro motion available. Camera-mode controls remain available in 3D scene settings.

Create a contact, open the three-dot Settings menu → Developer → 3D scene, and enable Cards in the list or Expanded card. Motion works with gyro alone. On a supported device, choose Use camera for face perspective, review the explanation, and continue to the iOS camera permission prompt. Denying permission leaves gyro motion available. The Enable camera in Settings button opens the app's iOS Settings page to restore access. Face tracking is unavailable on unsupported hardware, including devices without supported ARKit face tracking; those devices use gyro motion. No account or sign-in is required.
